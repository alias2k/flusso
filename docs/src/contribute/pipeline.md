# The pipeline

Two generic engines over three ports, and the invariants that make them correct: build once with several builds in grant order, write-then-ack with progress the engine keeps, several writes per sink with per-document order, sink-decided backfill, continuity before trust, at-least-once requests, and item rejections separated from write errors. Each is guarded by a named test.

## The shape

```text
                 ┌─ lane ─▶ SinkEngine(primary) ─▶ write ×4 ─▶ ack ─┐
ChangeCapture ─▶ IngestEngine ─┤                                       ├─▶ Progress ─▶ confirm
  resolve · build once · publish└─ lane ─▶ SinkEngine(audit)   ─▶ write ×4 ─▶ ack ─┘
                 ◀─ request lane ─ Backfill { sink, indexes } ─────────────────────
```

One source, one stream, N sinks. The **ingest engine** (`libs/2-engine/src/ingest.rs`) owns the source side: capture, resolve, build, publish, as futures of one task, a builder per index among them (`ingest/together.rs`: a wake between them only raises a flag, so a hand-over, a grant or a confirmation costs no trip through the scheduler; a poll makes at most 64 future polls, from a start that rotates every poll; the stop is watched once per run). Its **reader** (`ingest/reader.rs`) reads the live stream a run at a time (what is ready, up to `[batch] max_changes`, cut at `max_delay`), resolves each change, marks the documents it touches pending in the progress, and hands each index's builder the changes that touched its index (with one index, every change). Each **builder** (`ingest/builder.rs`, one per index with lanes) cuts its index's batches from the changes queued on it as one task did: everything read before `max_delay` past the later of the batch's first change and the previous grant, up to `max_changes` (one task stopped reading at a closed batch and took the backlog when the next grant reopened it), builds, publishes to its index's lanes and serves its index's backfills; one index's build takes at most `max_changes` documents, and what a capped take leaves is taken next, change or not. A builder the budget holds, or whose publish is blocked on a full lane (with other builders beside it), **folds**: it drops the changes queued on it, the reader hands it none, and it builds its waiting documents oldest first, `max_changes` at a time, until none waits, so a held index stops no other's reading. Builders share the build permits; with two or more, a batch that got no permit, or that the budget refused, waits in the gate's line until one is freed or the budget has room, so a younger builder reserves behind one the limit refused. A build gives its permit back as it ends, and a builder leaves every line while it awaits anything outside its loop (a publish blocked on a full lane, a backfill batch's walk marks) and whenever it ends, so it never holds a place it can't use. Backfill walks run at once, each builder walking its own index as soon as its requests are gathered; key reads share one permit, taken inside the read, and with two or more builders each read runs on a task of its own (aborted with its walk), so a builder that stops stepping its walk (a publish blocked on a full lane) holds no other walk's key reads. One builder reads inline. Its **dispatcher** (`ingest/dispatcher.rs`) owns the run's result: it forwards every backfill request ready on the request lane to its index's builder each time it is polled, never waiting on it; confirms once per poll in which something was registered (a run routed, a build's lanes, a release), and on a one-second tick; raises the builders at the stop and, once every builder is idle at the stream's end, at the end; and finishes the run: at a stop it waits until every lane acknowledged what was built and closes the live stream, at the stream's end it confirms once more. The reader runs ahead of the builds up to a **lead**: `[concurrency] max_waiting_per_index` documents waiting on one index (default `2 × max_changes`), `max_waiting` on every index together and in changes read ahead of the oldest change queued on a builder that doesn't fold (default `2 × builds × max_changes`); past it, it waits for a builder to take some. With two or more builders, an index whose builder is **held** (its live batch refused by the budget, or a publish pending on a full lane, until its next live grant or the publish going through) with `max_waiting_per_index` documents waiting is **exempt**: the reader latches it in its lead check, and clears it once fewer wait. Its documents count toward no other bound, only toward `[concurrency] max_pending_documents` over every exempt index (default 1,000,000), where the reading stops for every index. So a stuck index stops no other index's reading or writing; it keeps its changes as documents to build (each once, about 266 B) and keeps the slot pinned, so WAL grows. An exempt index grows only while it is held and some other index is free, since the exemption is there so that others keep going. An index is free while it is not held and nothing of it has waited on a lane for an acknowledgement for 5 s with none acknowledged (`ingest/outstanding.rs`: every published item, live or backfill, carries a guard in its `Hold` until every lane lets go of it); one guard per item, shared by its lanes, so a healthy sink acknowledging it doesn't free an index another sink holds; an index that publishes nothing is free. Once its hold ends (merely slow now), or no other index is free (a whole sink down, within about 5 s of each index's first unacknowledged item), it may drain but not grow; so a whole sink down stops the reading at `max_waiting` as it always did unless some index is silent (then the held ones read on to `max_pending_documents`, as one stuck index beside quiet healthy ones does), and a slow index paces the reading again once it drains below its cap. Either edge of a hold wakes the reader, and the dispatcher's one-second tick wakes a waiting reader whenever there are two or more builders, so it reads on once an index is acknowledged again. The exemption's log lines (per index: not exempt, exempt and growing, exempt and frozen; and the pending cap's) are checked on that tick only, for every index whatever the reader is doing, one a minute per index and one for the cap, the current state logged at the next tick once the minute is over; the engine keeps their state across runs (`ingest/reader/log.rs`), so within a minute and a second of any change the last line names the current state, except while no engine run is live. A build takes only the documents of the runs it covers, never a newer run's, so no change counts as committed before its documents land. The rules are R1–R10 of `docs/adr/0012-stage-notes/stage-8a.md` §3.8. One **sink engine** per sink (`sink_engine.rs`) owns delivery to that sink and that sink's seeding. The **stream** (`libs/1-ports/stream`) carries one lane per sink downward and a request lane upward. The engine's **progress** (`libs/2-engine/src/progress.rs`) records what was routed to each lane and has not landed; confirmation and fences are read off it. The **daemon** (`libs/3-daemon/src/supervise.rs`) stages every sink engine, then runs all engines as independent tasks. Everything an engine drives is a trait object, so backends swap without touching either loop.

## Documents are built once

The ingest engine buffers live changes per `BatchPolicy` (`[batch]` in `flusso.toml`; default 256 changes, 50 ms cap): it takes every change the stream has ready and commits the moment the stream would block, so a lone change on a quiet stream is built at once while a burst fills batches to `max_changes` (`1` reproduces a write per change). It resolves each to the `DocumentId`s it touches (an update whose pre- and post-image agree on every column a path reads resolves to nothing on that path; `source::relation_columns`/`root_columns` derive the columns, guarded by the `wal` e2e's `an_update_of_unread_columns_rebuilds_nothing` and the `pipeline` e2e's `an_update_of_an_unread_column_writes_nothing`), dedups them, and `build_many` assembles each touched document **once** even when several changes hit it and however many sinks there are. The result is published as one `Batch` of kernel `Envelope`s, tagged with the position of the last change, to every lane. A backfill (a *snapshot*) goes through the same build but is never resolved: it walks each requested index's root keys in pages (`DocumentBuilder::keys(index, after, limit)`, `engine::KEY_PAGE` = 1,024 at a time, the engine holding the last key read as the cursor), and each key is its own document of that index, built once (issue #385; resolving it like a live change cost a reverse query per related path and rebuilt related indexes' parents again and again). A page is read when the walk needs it, on the builder's pool, with no snapshot shared across pages (issue #386 stage 3): `prepare` made the slot before the first page, so a row that gets a key behind the walk reaches the live stream, and a page only names ids, each document read when it is built. The page future is kept in the snapshot, not in the `select!`, so a live change winning the `select!` drops no page half read. Guards: `tests::snapshot`'s `each_page_follows_the_last_key_the_walk_read`, the `keyset` e2e in `source-postgres` (every key type in key order, operators off the search path, a migration between pages) and the engine's `keyset_backfill` e2e (writes behind, ahead of and inside the page just read, a partitioned root, a stop mid-walk). They are published without a position and only to the lanes that requested them, ending with a `SnapshotComplete` marker. Both kinds interleave on one task, published in the order they were granted (below), so a document's later message is always its newer state. The sink engine keeps that order per document while it writes several items at once (below), so sinks need no versioning. Guards: `builds_a_repeatedly_touched_document_once_per_batch`, `batches_changes_into_a_single_build_and_flush`, `a_lone_change_on_a_quiet_stream_commits_without_waiting`, `max_delay_caps_a_batch_while_changes_keep_arriving`, `snapshots_go_only_to_the_requesting_lane`, `a_backfill_of_related_indexes_builds_each_document_once`, and the `backfill` e2e.

## At-least-once via write-then-ack and the progress

A position describes itself (ADR 0012): the source stamps each one as it emits the change, with how far it may confirm or fence while the change is the oldest outstanding (`confirm_before`, `fence_before`) and once it has landed (`confirm_through`), so the source keeps no map of what it emitted. The ingest engine **routes** a batch: it publishes it to every lane, then registers it in `Progress` with its oldest position, under the item's **key**: a number `Progress` hands out per index, the same on every lane of that index. A sink engine reports a lane item **landed** when its write returned with nothing refused for good, before it acks it, and how far its writes are searchable. From that `Progress` gives a `Landed` case: `Before(o)` while `o` is the oldest position some lane hasn't landed, `Through(p)` with nothing outstanding and `p` the newest position routed to the lane or by the reader, `Idle` before anything is routed. The reader raises its routed position after each run's marks (`Progress::routed`): a lane of a quiet index, with nothing waiting or building, answers through it, so it pins neither confirmation nor a fence while another index builds. An engine restart forgets it with the run's marks. The ingest engine hands it to `ChangeCapture::confirm` after each commit when it changed, and on a one-second tick **always**, since the source's answer moves with idle WAL while the case stays put. The source decides the rest: `Through(p)` follows its horizon only while `p` is the last change it emitted (an emitted change not yet routed is in no lane), and `Idle` only while it emitted nothing; otherwise it stops at `p`'s own commit, or does not move. So the slot advances past a change exactly when **every** sink has made its documents durable, and never into the record of a commit not yet landed. The ingest engine writes only what it registers and `routed`; a sink engine only its lane's reports; neither takes the other's lock. A sink engine that dies before a write returns leaves the item unacked; its lane redelivers it to the restarted engine, and the rebuild is idempotent (deterministic ids, current state); a repeated landing report is harmless. Positions strictly increase within one stream, and over the source's life a position never names a different change than it named before: `Progress` keeps each lane's newest position for its whole life. A lane's newest names only positions whose every document of its index was taken: a take cut by the document cap, or a folding builder's backlog take, registers its item at a position some of its documents still wait at, so registering it leaves the newest where it was until nothing at or before that position waits or is in another build. Otherwise an engine restart, which forgets what waits, would let the lane answer through documents never written, and confirmation and a fence would pass them. The Postgres capture keeps numbering across reopened streams, so its `next_seq` test stays exact across an ingest restart; a source may instead give a replayed change the position it had. Renumbering with new meanings would be unsafe. The status phase drops to `starting` while the ingest engine restarts and recovers when it follows the source again; only a clean end is `stopped`. Guards: `progress::tests` (`every_interleaving_answers_as_the_model_does`, `a_stalled_lane_pins_confirmation_and_its_own_fence_only`, `a_landing_reported_before_its_registration_still_counts`), `confirms_no_position_before_its_flush`, `redelivers_the_unacked_batch_to_a_restarted_sink_engine`, the channel adapter's `several_deliveries_can_be_outstanding_and_ack_independently`, the daemon's `failed_sink_engine_restarts_and_redelivers` and `failed_ingest_engine_restarts_and_resumes_the_stream`, the Postgres adapter's `ack::tests` (`a_change_emitted_but_not_routed_is_never_fenced_or_confirmed`, `the_tick_releases_idle_wal_while_routed_stays_put`, `a_fresh_process_never_confirms_its_first_unrouted_change`, `an_idle_stream_after_an_ingest_restart_still_releases_wal`, `a_reopened_stream_continues_the_numbering`).

**Pending documents** (ADR 0012 stage 8a). From the grant of a live build until a lane registers its item, the documents are on no lane, so `Progress` keeps them per index (`progress/pending.rs`). The ingest engine marks its run's documents pending (at the oldest change that touched each, dropping those of an index with no lane) and offers the batch to the gate only while none of them is in a running build: `main`'s admission, which a build leaves when it lands. The grant takes every index's waiting documents into one build under a token; a run that touched nothing a lane receives is an empty build, settled in slot order as before. Publishing marks the build as sending; each lane's registration (or skip) settles the build for that lane before the one notification that wakes a fence waiter, and the token is released once every lane has. A lane answers `Before` the oldest of its own unlanded items, a batch being sent that it hasn't taken (its own), and its index's backlog (waiting or building, not yet sent): a full sibling lane still holds back only itself. Stall age counts only what a lane holds itself; when the backlog is what holds the slot, `pinned_by` names the index alone, and on a tie the lane is named. What a failed run left unpublished is forgotten when the next run starts: confirmation stayed before it, so the replayed stream marks it again, batched as it is read. A live batch carries its own live run's newest change, as before, so positions grow along a lane in grant order, and across runs the position contract above holds. A batch never borrows another change's position, which would let a lane that never got that change answer through it.

## Several builds at once, published in order

The ingest engine runs up to `builds` `build_many` calls at once (`[concurrency] builds`, default 4; ADR 0012 stage 4, `ingest/builds.rs`), still from its one task. Once concurrent writes landed, the serial build was what bounded a backfill.

- **The gate.** Two batches can wait for a build slot: the live batch (once the stream would block or the batch closed) and the snapshot's (once full, or its walk is done). Live goes first, except every fourth grant both could take. The count moves only on such contested grants. A second live build starts only while the batch behind the first is full, so a trickle still batches itself, and a lone change on a quiet stream is granted the moment the stream would block. With one builder, a build asks the gate in one pass: it joins the line of each kind it has a batch ready for and a slot to run, asks once, and leaves before it moves on. With several builders (one per index), a batch the pass didn't grant keeps its place in the line while the batch is ready and a slot is free, asked again with its current size whenever the gate changes (a batch that grows keeps a refusal, one that shrinks has it cleared), so a freed permit has someone to wake and every-fourth is contested across builders. A freed permit goes to the oldest eligible waiter by the same rule, the budget is asked at grant (a refused waiter keeps its place and the permit goes on), and a dropped waiter leaves its line. A waiter behind an older one the limit refused takes no room by the limit; it is asked again as soon as nothing holds it back: the older one left, was refused by its share instead, or the budget changed. While anyone waits, every change of what the budget holds moves its generation, so a refusal is always read as the budget would answer now. Key reads (a page or an index's bound) share one permit across walks, held while the read runs, so one walk reads at a time, as before.
- **Per document.** A batch is granted only when no running build holds one of its documents (64-bit id hashes, kept sorted per build; a collision only holds a batch back; nothing is hashed at `builds = 1`). So a later build of a document starts after the earlier one returned, and under Read Committed it reads newer state.
- **Grant order is lane order.** Every grant takes the next slot in a queue (at most `2 × builds` slots, running or built); the front is published once it is ready, so a lane gets items in grant order whatever finishes first. Live positions only grow along a lane and in `Progress`, which registers them in that order. A snapshot's `SnapshotComplete` takes a slot of its own, after its last batch.
- **Running a build.** A build is polled once where it is granted; one that doesn't finish then is spawned on its own task, so its decoding and scripts run beside the engine and it progresses while the engine publishes. Spawned builds are awaited through `Flights`. The reader reads and resolves changes while builds run, up to its lead.
- **Failure and stop.** A failed build, or a document failed under `stop`, stops every grant; the slots before it are published, nothing from it on, and the run awaits every running build before returning the error. A stop, and the end of the live stream, first wait for the reader to hand over what it read and end; then every run it handed over is built in batches once its documents are free, and every build is finished and published in order. A reader error ends the run with it; a builder error ends the run and aborts the reader.

Guards: the engine's `ingest::tests` (`random_schedules_keep_every_document_in_order`, a seeded random schedule with failures and restarts that `FLUSSO_BUILD_SCHEDULE_SEED` replays; `builds_run_at_once_up_to_the_setting`; `a_document_is_never_built_twice_at_once`; `every_fourth_contested_grant_goes_to_the_backfill`; `a_failed_build_publishes_nothing_after_it`; `a_document_failed_under_stop_publishes_nothing_after_it_and_skip_goes_on`; `a_stop_finishes_the_builds_in_flight_and_starts_no_other`; `a_lone_change_on_a_quiet_stream_still_commits_at_once`; `one_build_at_a_time_builds_and_publishes_as_before`, recorded from the serial engine).

## Built documents within a byte budget

The ingest engine holds built documents within `[concurrency] max_built_mib` (default 512 MiB; ADR 0012 stage 5, `engine::Budget` in `libs/2-engine/src/budget.rs`), from their build until every lane acknowledged them.

- **Counted.** A build's documents are measured by a walk of their trees on the build's own task (`document_bytes`: strings, byte arrays, arrays, B-tree nodes; held to a counting allocator within 0.8–1.2 by `tests/heap_walk.rs`), at least 1 KiB a batch.
- **Reserved, then settled, per index.** A grant estimates each index's part (its ids × a running average per document of that index and kind) in `Builds::choose`, asks `Budget::admit`, lets the gate pick, then reserves with `Budget::reserve`, which checks again and adds in one step under the budget's lock, so several reservers never pass the limit together. The build settles each part to the measured size of that index's documents when it lands. A candidate the budget can't fit is simply not admissible: the gate's every-fourth counter doesn't move, and the loop waits for a release.
- **Released by the last copy.** Each index's settled `Arc<Charge>` rides that index's lane pieces as `stream::Batch.hold`. The channel's ledger keeps a copy until the acknowledgement, so the bytes come back exactly when every lane of the index acknowledged the piece and nothing can redeliver it. A release takes the lock for a few subtractions, bumps a generation and wakes every waiter after letting it go; a waiter passes the generation of its refusal to `Budget::released_since`, so a release between the refusal and the wait still wakes it.
- **Rules.** Total within the limit; backfill within half plus one batch per index walking (below), so live keeps room under the limit, less as many indexes walk with large batches, and rule 5 still gives every index one live batch whatever the backfills hold; with two or more indexes, one index's **live** bytes within `[concurrency] index_budget_share` of the limit (default half); an index holding no backfill bytes is always granted one backfill batch, so a walk stuck behind a parked lane, holding backfill's half, leaves every other walk going; and an index holding no live bytes is always granted one live batch, past the limit and its share if need be, so a batch larger than the budget still builds, alone, live holding nothing takes every index waiting, and one index stuck at its share (a parked lane, for hours) leaves the others building and, once it has `max_waiting_per_index` documents waiting, becomes exempt, so the reading goes on for them too, up to `max_pending_documents` (above). An index's backfill bytes don't count against its share (backfill is capped as a whole), so an index being backfilled or reindexed keeps, for its live changes, the room live had before the share. A batch, backfill or live, fits whole or not at all: each builder builds one index, so an index the budget refuses holds only its own builder (which folds, above), never another index's batch. Builders wait in the gate's line in the order they asked, and the first refused by the limit keeps younger ones that hold live bytes from taking room by it, so small batches can't starve a large one; an index holding none still gets its one batch. One index's live build takes at most `max_changes` documents, and its grant reserves for no more. A live wait for the budget (`flusso.memory.refused` and `flusso.memory.wait`) lasts while any builder is held: one index held at its share while others build is one refusal and one wait, and overlapping holds of several builders are one wait too. Held stays within the limit plus one live and one backfill batch per index, and what each batch in flight settles past its estimate; one index's live bytes within its share (or one larger batch) plus the same. Whether a hold was counted belongs to the engine, its wait to the engine run: a run that ends reports its open wait up to then, and the next run's refusal of the same hold starts a new wait without counting again.
- **Lanes.** `[stream] capacity` is deprecated: lanes built from configuration keep a backstop of 1,048,576 items, which the 1 KiB floor keeps from binding before the budget. With lanes that don't fill, `publish` doesn't block the loop; a stalled sink is felt as the budget filling, while the loop goes on landing builds and serving what has room.

Guards: `budget::tests`, `heap_walk`, and `ingest::tests`' `random_schedules_under_a_tiny_budget_stay_within_it_and_finish`, `random_schedules_over_three_indexes_with_one_bursting_stay_within_their_shares`, `one_bursting_index_of_three_holds_its_share_and_the_others_build`, `an_index_held_at_its_share_is_one_wait_however_many_builds_pass_it`, `an_index_backfilling_behind_a_slow_sink_keeps_both_live_streams_building`, `a_parked_index_at_its_share_leaves_a_healthy_index_writing_for_ten_minutes`, `a_hold_across_an_engine_restart_is_one_wait`, `a_wait_open_when_an_engine_run_ends_is_reported_up_to_that_moment`, `a_deferred_index_is_never_counted_committed_before_its_documents_land`, `an_index_held_past_its_waiting_bound_is_exempt_and_the_reading_goes_on`, `a_stalled_sink_paces_builds_and_they_resume_when_it_does`, `live_keeps_room_beside_a_backfill_holding_its_half`, `a_batch_bigger_than_the_budget_still_builds`, `a_batch_redelivered_after_a_failed_write_gives_its_bytes_back_once_written`.

## Several writes per sink, one document at a time

A sink engine writes up to `writes` lane items at once (`[concurrency] writes`, default 4; ADR 0012 stage 2). Writing is the measured bottleneck: OpenSearch takes overlapping bulk requests about as well on one index as across several.

- **The window.** The engine takes an item from the lane only to use it: while a write slot is free, or, with only backfill waiting, to find a live item behind it, up to 32 waiting. A delivered item leaves the lane's bound, so each sink holds at most `writes + 32` delivered items beside the lane's `capacity`, about `writes` with live traffic alone. A granted write is started at once, so one that completes on its first poll is acknowledged in the same turn, as one write at a time was. With nothing waiting and no room to take more, the run waits only for a write landing, a control message or a position coming due, as one write at a time waited inside its flush: nothing can be taken from the lane, a stop changes only waiting backfill (there is none), and the engine isn't idle. One still running waits in `Flights` (`sink_engine/flights.rs`), which polls a write only when its own waker fired and never wakes the run by itself: `FuturesUnordered` woke its task after every pass that left all its futures pending, one empty wake-up for each write that had to wait, about 1 µs of CPU per lane item at one change per batch.
- **Admission.** An item starts only when no earlier unfinished item shares a document (`(index, id)`, hashed) with it. So a document is never written twice at once, and a later build lands after an earlier one: the per-document order the ingest engine's lane order gives.
- **Merging.** A write holds every item ready and admissible at once, of the kind the grant chose, across lanes and several of one lane, up to `[batch] max_changes` documents and the sink's `write_limit` (OpenSearch: `batch_size`), never splitting an item: `Sink::write_parts`. A live item that is ready while a write is out waits for it, at most `[batch] max_delay_ms` and never for a write already out that long, so both go out as one request (stage-7 §14; guards: `tests::wait`). A burst over several indexes goes out in writes of about a batch, not one per index. Each part is acknowledged, fenced and decided on its own: a `stop` rejection holds back and blames only its own lane. A lane behind a barrier, and an ordered sink, write one item alone. Rules: `docs/adr/0012-stage-notes/stage-7.md` §12; guards: `tests::merge`.
- **Selection.** Live items (they carry a position) go before backfill items, except every fourth grant both could take, which goes to the oldest admissible backfill item. A `SnapshotComplete` runs once everything before it on its lane is written: it seeds its index (`mark_seeded`) as a flight holding one write slot, and nothing after it on its lane starts until the seed returned. Other lanes keep writing and seeding meanwhile, so the seeds at the end of a backfill overlap each other and the writes still going. A seed that fails fails the run like a failed write. A reindex control message waits for the writes and seeds in flight.
- **Fences in lane order.** A lane's live items are kept by their key. *Applied* is reported to `Progress` per item when its write returns, before its acknowledgement. *Searchable* trails it in the same order, each item due by what its own write reported. Observers still hear the item's position.
- **Caught up.** A write is caught up when nothing else is queued or waiting. A caught-up write makes every write that returned before it searchable (the `Sink::write` contract). Before the acknowledgement that leaves the engine idle, a position not yet searchable gets an empty caught-up write, so the last writes of a burst don't wait out `refresh_interval`.
- **Failure.** A failed write starts nothing new (a `stop` rejection, a write block or a missing target parks or restages its lane instead, see [Rejections, write-wide errors and parked lanes](#rejections-write-wide-errors-and-parked-lanes)): the writes in flight are awaited and acknowledged when they succeeded, then the run ends with the error, and the lane redelivers only what is unacknowledged. The next run is a **barrier**, and so is the first run of a fresh engine (a previous process may have stopped on its first item): it writes the oldest item alone and opens the window only once a write landed, so a write that fails every time halts the sink as one write at a time did (nothing behind it lands, no run makes progress, the backoff grows, the lane fills). A fresh engine's oldest item is the oldest unconfirmed one: when that is an older batch already written (a sibling sink lags, or the confirmation never reached the source), it lands, the window opens, and disjoint batches past the rejected one, granted beside it or while it is in flight, can land before it fails. Nothing is lost. Guards: the engine's `after_a_failed_run_the_oldest_item_is_written_alone_until_it_lands`, the daemon's `a_stop_rejection_halts_its_lane_writing_several_items_at_once`.

Guards: the engine's `tests::concurrent` (`a_document_is_never_written_twice_at_once_and_its_versions_never_go_down`, a seeded random schedule that `FLUSSO_SCHEDULE_SEED` replays; `a_later_write_finishing_first_moves_neither_confirmation_nor_the_fence`; `live_goes_first_except_every_fourth_contested_grant`; `a_snapshot_complete_is_a_barrier`; `a_reindex_waits_for_the_writes_in_flight`; `tests::seeding`'s seeds at once, a failed seed, a stop and a reindex beside a seed; `a_failed_write_lets_the_others_finish_and_comes_back_alone`; `a_stop_finishes_the_writes_in_flight_and_skips_waiting_backfill`; `an_idle_engine_makes_its_last_writes_searchable`), `tests::busy`'s `a_busy_item_holds_back_only_the_items_sharing_its_documents`, and the OpenSearch sink's `concurrent_writes_each_send_their_own_documents` and `a_caught_up_write_refreshes_what_earlier_writes_left`.

## Backfill is each sink's decision

Staging (`SinkEngine::stage`) `ensure_index`es every mapping, asks the sink `is_seeded` per index, and sends one `Backfill { sink, indexes }` request up the request lane for the unseeded set. The indexes are staged concurrently, `STAGE_CONCURRENCY` (8) at a time, a fixed bound because OpenSearch applies index creations and alias changes one cluster-state update at a time, so more in flight gains nothing (4, 8 and 16 measured alike on a fresh cluster): each index's own steps stay in order, and nothing orders one index against another, since each has its own target. The `Sink` port says so: `ensure_index`, `is_seeded` and `reindex` may run at once for different indexes, never for the same one, and beside a `write`/`mark_seeded` of other indexes only (a lane staged again after its target went missing). Once an index fails no further index is started (a cluster that is down or never answers fails every index alike, and each further wave would cost another request timeout); the ones in flight finish, the error (`EngineError::Staging`) names every failure seen with its own error, and the daemon retries the whole stage, which starts the rest. A reindex control naming an index twice stages it once. The ingest engine walks the union of the requested indexes and publishes their documents to the requesting lanes only; a seeded sibling sees nothing, so a stateless sink beside OpenSearch no longer re-seeds it on every restart. `backfill = false` on a sink skips staging's request entirely and never marks anything seeded. Requests coalesce into one snapshot fanned to every requester: whatever is already queued is taken with no wait (every startup request is, since the daemon stages every sink before the ingest engine runs), and the engine holds for stragglers only while some lane has not asked, for at most the batch policy's `max_delay`, so a reindex fanned to every sink is one pass over the table and a single-sink backfill starts at once. The builder gathers them on a deadline beside its live work, so live batches are built and published while it waits (`coalescing_for_one_index_delays_no_live_change_of_it`). Requests are at-least-once: the ingest engine acks a request only after its `SnapshotComplete` is published, so a crash mid-snapshot redelivers it; and a sink engine remembers which indexes it has requested and not yet seen seeded, so re-staging after its own restart does not ask twice while the index's target is still the one its backfill was asked for (see below). Guards: `an_unseeded_sink_requests_a_snapshot_then_marks_seeded`, `a_seeded_sink_requests_nothing`, `backfill_false_makes_a_stateless_sink_live_only`, `concurrent_requests_for_the_same_index_coalesce_into_one_snapshot`, the daemon's `unseeded_sink_is_backfilled_without_touching_its_sibling`; for the concurrency, the engine's `tests::staging` (the bound, one index's steps in order and never overlapping, one request for the unseeded set, every failure seen named, no index started after a failure, a reindex naming an index twice) and the OpenSearch sink's `many_indexes_staged_at_once_each_get_their_own_generation` e2e (24 at once on a fresh cluster, where the first meta writes race to create `flusso_meta`, then a restart, then rebuilds).

### A backfill whose target changed can't seed it

A target deleted or recreated mid-backfill holds only the pages written after it came back, so the backfill that was writing into it must not seed it. Two facts tell the engine:

- **Tickets.** Each `Request::Backfill` carries a `stream::Ticket`, numbered up per sink engine. The ingest engine answers a lane's requests in one snapshot under the greatest ticket it serves, named on every snapshot `Batch` and on the `SnapshotComplete`.
- **Target identity.** `Sink::ensure_index` answers `Ensured { target, created }`: a `TargetId` naming the target the index writes to (OpenSearch: the generation's `index.uuid`), which a target created anew never shares.

The sink engine keeps, per outstanding index, the ticket and target it asked under. Right after `ensure_index`, another target, `created` when either side names none, or any restage (it runs only after a write found the target missing) **cuts** the backfill. The cut sticks until a fresh request is published, so a staging that fails after finding it loses nothing. While cut, no page of the old request records a checkpoint, its `SnapshotComplete` is acknowledged without seeding, and every staging asks again from the first key for the current target. The old snapshot's remaining pages are still written, in lane order; the fresh walk writes them again. A seed the sink refuses with `SinkError::TargetChanged` stages the lane again, which cuts.

An ordinary restart, with the target intact, still asks nothing twice. Guards: the engine's `tests::gone`, `walked::tests`, and the OpenSearch sink's `reindex` e2e (`a_generation_deleted_mid_backfill_is_staged_as_created_without_its_checkpoint`, `an_auto_created_generation_is_never_seeded_and_is_replaced_by_a_strict_one`, `a_seeded_generation_recreated_by_a_staging_that_then_failed_is_never_served_seeded`).

## A seed is only as good as the stream behind it

Run order is `source.continuity()` → stage every sink → `source.prepare()` → serve requests and live. `continuity` is read-only (Postgres: does the slot exist?) and answers `Resumed` or `Fresh`; `prepare` creates the resume point and is the ingest engine's first act. On `Fresh`, every change since the last seed is unobservable, so each sink engine warns and stages `sink.reindex` plus `ensure_index` for every index still `is_seeded` (a fresh generation, so rows gone from the source drop on the swap, never an in-place reseed), and the normal backfill refills them.

The order is load-bearing twice. Rebuilds are staged **before** `prepare` creates the slot, so a crash in between comes back `Fresh` and re-stages instead of `Resumed` trusting stale seeds. `prepare` runs **before** any backfill page, so a write a page doesn't see is in the stream. The daemon enforces it by finishing every sink's first staging before it starts the ingest engine. Only seeded indexes are staged; an unseeded one is already being rebuilt and staging again would orphan a generation. Under `--skip-backfill` staging only warns. Guards: `fresh_source_rebuilds_seeded_indexes_before_prepare_then_snapshots`, `fresh_source_leaves_an_unseeded_index_to_the_normal_backfill`, `skip_backfill_with_a_fresh_source_stages_nothing`, the daemon's `fresh_source_rebuilds_seeded_sinks_before_preparing` and `every_index_of_every_sink_is_staged_before_the_source_prepares`, the sink's `deleted_generation_is_recreated_and_reported_unseeded` e2e, the Postgres `continuity` e2e, and the two `restart_*` cases in the engine's `pipeline` e2e.

## Reindex is an operation on one sink

A reindex reaches a running sink engine as a control message from the daemon (`DaemonControl::reindex(index, Option<sink>)`, behind `POST /indexes/<name>/reindex`). Once the writes in flight have returned, the engine stages a fresh generation (`reindex` + `ensure_index`, several indexes at once as at start) and sends a new `Backfill` request; the current generation keeps serving until the snapshot completes and `SnapshotComplete` lets the sink swap. Nothing restarts, and untargeted sinks are untouched; forcing every sink coalesces into one snapshot. A sink with `backfill = false` ignores it (nothing to rebuild), as does an index whose snapshot is still outstanding. The OpenSearch sink's own `reindex` also stages nothing for an index that is still unseeded, so a second reindex can't orphan the generation being built. Guards: `reindex_control_stages_and_requests_a_snapshot_without_restarting`, the daemon's `reindex_operation_targets_one_sink`.

## A stop is a drain

A stop is a drain, not an abort. The daemon hands the ingest engine an `engine::Stop`, which the reader checks between the live stream's items, never by cancelling a task: it reads nothing more, hands over what it read and ends; the builder waits for that, drops a snapshot in progress unacked (the sink asks again on the next start), builds and publishes everything the reader read (bounded by the lead, plus every exempt index's documents, bounded by `max_pending_documents`), in batches of `max_changes`, waits with `drain_lanes` (the same drain that runs when the live stream ends) until every lane is acked, confirms what landed, and asks the source to close its stream once the position reached the server (`ChangeCapture::close_live`). A stalled sink holds the drain; the binary's deadline bounds it, and giving up is safe for the reason above: the exit is 1, and the log names each index left with documents read and not built. No run ends `Ok` with a document waiting (`EngineError::DocumentsLeft`). The user-facing sequence is [Stopping](../reference/cli.md#stopping). Guards: the engine's and the daemon's `tests::stop`.

## Rejections, write-wide errors and parked lanes

`Sink::write` returns a `WriteReport`, or `Err`. Each outcome has its own owner:

| Outcome | Example | Who handles it | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Err` | the destination unreachable, the whole request refused | the daemon | The sink engine's run ends; it restarts with backoff. Every lane of the sink waits. |
| `rejected` (per document) | a mapping conflict | `FailurePolicies`, by logical index | `Skip` quarantines it and acks the part; `Stop` **parks** the lane. |
| `blocked` (per index) | a write block, an item-level `security_exception` | the sink engine | The lane **parks**, whatever the policy. |
| `missing` (per index) | a generation deleted while running | the sink engine | The lane is **staged again**. |
| busy items (429/503) | a full write queue | the sink, inside `write` | Resent; never reported. |

### A write-wide error restarts the sink

An `Err` stops **that sink engine** once the writes in flight beside it returned. The daemon restarts it on the one retry schedule, `engine::Backoff` ([`[backoff]`](../reference/config-toml.md#backoff), resolved and checked by `daemon::backoff`): the first wait, doubling to the longest, back to the first after a run that made progress. Progress means:

- the sink engine's oldest unfinished lane item landed (a later item landing beside an older one that then fails is no progress);
- or the ingest engine moved its source forward: its source reported reading further than ever, keepalives on a quiet stream included (or, for a source that reports no such position, confirming further than ever);
- or a backfill finished.

Publishing alone is not progress: a restart publishes a replayed change or backfill page again. The ingest engine and the other sinks keep running meanwhile. The failed sink's lane fills, so with the in-process stream a stalled sink eventually paces ingest and pins WAL; its per-sink lag and in-flight numbers are the alarm.

### A rejection is decided by its policy

`FailurePolicies` (a global default plus per-index overrides, resolved by logical name) decides each rejection when the write returns. `Skip` quarantines it (`Observer::on_document_quarantined`, with the sink) and acks the part, so the poison isn't redelivered. `Stop` parks the lane (ADR 0012 stage 9). The OpenSearch sink never retries a rejection, a block or a missing target inside a write.

### A parked lane holds itself, not the sink

A parked lane's parts go back in the sink engine's window unacknowledged, in receive order. Its marks, its fences and the slot stay where they are. Nothing of it is granted: it takes nothing more from its lane, and its items neither hold nor end a merge wait. Other lanes, and the merged write's other parts, land and are acknowledged as usual.

When its backoff is due, its front item is written alone: a **probe**. The wait is the same `engine::Backoff` (`SinkEngine::with_backoff`); only that lane's probe landing resets it.

- **Landed:** the lane opens and the rest follows.
- **Refused or blocked again:** the backoff doubles.
- **A stop:** every parked lane is probed once more at once.

### A missing target is staged again, alone

A restaging lane waits until nothing of it is in flight. Then it runs `ensure_index` for its index alone, beside the other lanes' writes (the port allows staging beside writes of other indexes), then `seeding` and a `Backfill` request when the index is unseeded and none is outstanding for its current target (a restage always cuts the backfill in flight, see [A backfill whose target changed can't seed it](#a-backfill-whose-target-changed-cant-seed-it)). Then it opens and writes its items again. A failed staging is retried with the same backoff, and a lane reopened by staging keeps that backoff until one of its writes lands, so a target staging can't cure is never a loop.

### The lane's state outlives a run

The lane's state (`LaneHealth`: open, parked, restaging, with its backoff; `/status` splits parked into `refused` and `blocked` by its `ParkReason`) lives on the `SinkEngine`, not on the run. A run that fails for another reason leaves a parked lane parked, on its schedule; `stage` reopens a restaging lane, having ensured every index. The run ends only for a sink-wide failure: a write `Err`, a failed seed, a lane failing to deliver or acknowledge.

### Busy items stay inside the sink

An item refused as **busy** (429 or 503) is neither rejected nor blocked. The sink resends those items alone inside the same `write`, within `max_retries`, and fails the write when they are still refused. So the item stays unacked, and no later item sharing a document starts, until every document in it is written or rejected for what it holds. The retry lives in the sink because only it knows which items to resend and that the rest are already applied; the engine sees a slower write or an `Err`. A write keeps nothing between calls, so a failed one leaves nothing behind for the redelivered item to duplicate.

### Guards

- **Policies:** `skip_policy_*`, `stop_policy_*`, `per_index_stop_*`.
- **Parking:** `tests::parking` (paused time: a refusal parks only its lane while the sibling lands and is fenced, probes and backoff, a lifted block opens the lane and a block quarantines nothing under either policy, a missing target restages one index, merged writes, `writes = 1` and an ordered sink, a stop's probe, a stop with a lane still blocked, parked across a failed run), `health::tests`, and the `lane_parking` live e2e (a write block under `stop` and `skip`, a generation deleted mid-run).
- **The sink's classification:** `bulk::tests`' `a_write_block_is_blocked_never_rejected`, `an_item_level_security_exception_is_blocked`, `a_flood_stage_block_is_busy_not_blocked`, `a_missing_alias_is_its_index_missing_not_a_failed_write`.
- **Busy:** the sink's `items_refused_as_busy_are_resent_until_written` and the engine's `a_batch_is_not_acked_until_its_busy_documents_are_written`.

## Observability is a trait

Both engines report lifecycle and progress to an `Observer` (`observer.rs`): sync, cheap, no-op by default, set with `with_observer`. Every sink-side event carries the `SinkName`; engine errors carry an `EngineId` (`Ingest` or `Sink(name)`). The daemon fans events to its per-sink `Status`; the binary attaches an OpenTelemetry observer. Guard: `reports_lifecycle_and_progress_to_the_observer`.

**What an operator reads of each index** (ADR 0012 stage 8a, slice 7) is not events either. The ingest engine keeps one `IndexStates` (`index_states.rs`) for its life and the daemon's `Status` reads it as a handle, as it reads `Progress`; it is written only at edges, never on the path a change takes:

- **exemption** (`none`, `exempt`, `frozen`) on the dispatcher's 1 s tick, from the rule the reader decides by (`Lead::deciding`), with the time it entered that state;
- **held by the budget** in the builder's loop, a lock only when the live batch's refusal starts or ends (one atomic load otherwise);
- **held by a lane** while a publish pends on a full lane, with the sink, through a guard dropped when the publish goes through (every publish of the engine's, a snapshot's end included, goes through `IngestEngine::publish_to`);
- **walking** from a snapshot's start until it is dropped (completed, stopped or failed), with the sinks it serves.

An engine run that ends, by any path, clears the exemption and the budget hold of every index: nothing is read between runs. A hold is information. `working` is about progress: false while items of the index wait on a lane and none was acknowledged for 5 s, read from the exemption's record (`ingest/outstanding.rs`) with two or more indexes and from the lanes' stall ages with one, which keeps no record; a held index whose sink keeps acknowledging works. `Status` adds `documents_waiting` (`Progress::waiting_on`) and `backlog_seconds`: how long ago the index's oldest unbuilt change was read. `Stalls` keeps, each second, how many changes had been read, and finds the first sample that had read `Progress::oldest_unbuilt`'s read number; 0 while no engine run is live, and forgotten when a run goes live or fails. It grows only while the index is stuck or exempt: reading stops at an index's cap, so an index that paces the reader stays within its read-ahead (seconds) at its sink's limit and however far it falls behind the source. That lag is the index's lanes' WAL behind (`flusso_lane_wal_behind_bytes`) and the slot lag. `pacing_reader` says the reader is stopped at this index's cap now, and the reader's waits at the `per_index` bound carry the index. Only enabled indexes have an entry. `/status`'s `ingest.indexes.<name>` and `GET /indexes/<name>/status` serve the same entry; the binary's `flusso.index.*` gauges read it at collection. The reader reports each wait at its lead with the bound that stopped it (`Observer::on_reader_wait`, from `Lead::reached_by`), the one event here. Guards: `index_states::tests`, `ingest::tests::states`, `reader::tests`' `the_lead_names_*`, `status::tests`, the daemon's `the_status_reads_each_index_from_the_ingest_engine`.

A sink's `changes_committed` is not an event: `Status` reads it from `Progress` when asked (`Progress::committed`). It counts changes read, per sink, once every lane of the sink has landed them or needed nothing for them, in read order.

- **Read numbers.** The reader numbers every live change it reads. A document marked pending keeps the read number of the oldest change that touched it.
- **A frontier per index.** At each live grant an index's lanes are registered with that index's frontier. With documents still waiting after the take, it is one below the oldest of their read numbers; with none waiting, it is every change read. A lane has committed the frontier of the newest entry it landed, with every item before it.
- **The catch-up.** A lane with nothing unlanded, whose index has nothing waiting and no build taken and not yet released, has committed every change read. So a change that builds nothing counts as soon as it is read and everything before it landed.
- **Never down.** Each lane keeps the highest value it answered; a sink's count is the least over its lanes, and a sink with no lane has committed every read.
- **A run cut before its marks.** When a reader error cuts a run, its reads are counted as read and never marked. From that moment, no frontier and no catch-up passes the first of them, through the restart's backoff.
- **Across an engine restart.** The ended run's least unbuilt read (a document waiting, a build never released, a run cut before its marks) caps each lane's catch-up until a step is registered on that lane after the restart, or until a step closes while its index has nothing waiting or building (a quiet index gets no step of its own). Those reads count no sooner than the first step registered on the lane, or, for a quiet index, the first step any builder closes after the restart while the index has nothing waiting or building; reads before the cap count at once. The restart forgets the run in one step under the registry lock: a reader sees the run whole or forgotten, never its documents gone while its routed position still answers.

A stalled lane holds its sink's count, as it holds confirmation. A redelivered item that had landed moves nothing, and a change a source replays after an ingest restart is a second read. Guards: the daemon's `tests::committed`; `progress::tests::{committed, frontier, restart, model, parked, forgetting, cut}`.
